How the core depths were read (important)
- The core reports are graphic logs. Only the core number, the cut interval and the recovery are
  printed as numbers. The descriptions stand beside a depth scale with a lithology column and a
  grain-size curve. Bed limits inside a core were therefore read from the page pictures (position
  of the lithology symbols and of the grain-size curve against the 5 m depth marks) and are good to
  about 0.5 m only. Each such row says so in its note. Core tops, and core bases taken from the
  printed recovery, are exact.
- In core 4 the report itself names two description depths ("as for 2603 m", "as for 2609 m") and in
  core 6 one ("as for 2638 m"); these agree with the positions read from the pictures.
- Core 1: 0.5 m recovered (2550.5-2551.0 m), of which only 10 cm were described (a 5 cm claystone
  piece and a 5 cm sandstone piece). The place of the pieces in the recovered interval is not given.
  Both rows carry the recovered interval 2550.5-2551.0 m and overlap; confidence low.
- Where a description is repeated or not renewed below a thin bed (core 5 below 2625 m, core 8 on
  its second sheet) the row uses the description that the lithology column continues.
- Thin beds that the grain-size curve shows but that have no words of their own were not given rows:
  about 2696.5-2697.5 m (silt bed in core 7 sandstone, kept as second class), about 2592 m (finer bed
  in core 3 sandstone) and the clay bed at about 2582-2583 m inside the laminated sandstone of core 3.

Class choices that a checker may want to look at
- "v arg/kao" sandstone. Core 2 (2552-2569.37 m) and the sandstone lens in core 1 are described as
  "Sst: ... v arg/kao", and the lithostratigraphy text for 2540 - 2569 m writes "very
  argillaceous/kaolinitic matrix". By the word map (sandstone called very argillaceous ->
  Sandstone/Shale) these rows are written as Sandstone/Shale. The words describe the matrix, the
  lithology column of the core report draws plain sandstone and the core table calls core 2
  "Sandstone", so Sandstone is the other possible reading. Rows concerned: 2540-2569 (interval
  summary), 2552-2552.5, 2552.5-2568.5, 2568.5-2569.37 (core). Sandstones described with
  "arg/kao mtrx" without "very" (cores 1, 3, 4, 9) and "Tr kaol/arg Mtrx" (cores 7, 8) are Sandstone.
- Siltstone is written as Shale with Sandstone/Shale as second class, as the word map says. In the
  Dunlin row (3177-3225 m) the second class is Sandstone instead, because sandstone is named there
  as beds.
- "minor tuff" (Balder), "minor limestone stringers" (Ty), "minor siltstone" (Oseberg): the minor
  rock is kept as second class, no row of its own.
- Formation summaries that name several rocks without shares have confidence low: Svarte
  (limestone interbedded with claystone, both said to grade to marl), Rodby, Heather C, Ness, Rannoch.
- Heather A (2727 - 3005 m): a limestone is described under the formation but is not named in the
  head sentence and has no depths; it is only mentioned in the note of that row.
